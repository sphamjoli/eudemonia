What does it take for an institution to TRUST a blockchain-based payment?

Speed and cost are solved.
The remaining constraint is trust: prove the required checks happened before payment, keep sensitive data private, and keep an audit trail.

Compliance infrastructure, not a consumer dApp
Scenario

Alice works in Johannesburg.
Her employer is in Amsterdam.

1
Salary crosses borders. SWIFT. 1–5 days. FX fees and spreads vary by month.
2
Each payment requires documented compliance: KYC, approvals, audit trail. Evidence is scattered across PDFs, tickets, and spreadsheets.
3
Alice is verified. The transfer goes through. No machine-verifiable proof travels with the payment.
The proof and the payment live in different systems. That gap repeats at scale.
One payment. Three blockers every institution hits when moving value on public rails.
Observed constraints

Three blockers

01
Privacy blocks coordination
Institutions avoid shared rails when counterparties, payment flows, or positions become visible. They will not expose sensitive operational data just to use new infrastructure. Privacy is a requirement, not a nice-to-have.
02
Compliance breaks across the workflow
Compliance is not one check. It spans request → approval → settlement → audit. Most systems cover one stage well, but not all stages together with privacy preserved. Teams bridge the gaps manually.
03
Trust is operational, not just technical
The bottleneck is not throughput. Institutions need enforceable controls and replayable audit evidence that map to current governance and regulation.
Hypothesis

Put proof of process on-chain, keep raw data off-chain.

Existing compliance providers still run checks. Approvers still approve. Eudemonia adds a verifiable proof layer so settlement happens only when rules are satisfied.

Under the hood

Four building blocks, explained simply.

Each block has one job. Together they ensure rules are enforced before payment.

keccak256
Digital fingerprint
All issuance parameters — amount, beneficiary, chain, policy — are hashed on submission and stored on-chain. If any field changes, the fingerprint changes. The circuit recomputes it; a mismatch means proof failure.
ECDSA · secp256k1
Signed approval
Attestors sign the parameters hash off-chain. The circuit recovers the signing address and checks it against the registered set. Only an authorised key can produce a valid signature.
Merkle inclusion proof
Proof of list membership
Attestors are registered as leaves in an on-chain Merkle tree. The circuit proves each signer is in that approved list without publishing the whole list each time — threshold is enforced with minimal disclosure.
SP1 zkVM · SNARK
Proof that rules were executed
The zkVM proves the validation logic ran correctly: checks passed, threshold met, records fresh. On-chain verification cost stays stable even as checks or approvers grow.
Step 1 of 5
make demo-setup

Step 1: the employer publishes a policy rulebook on-chain.

Before Alice can be paid, the employer defines the rules: who can attest, how many approvals are required, what compliance checks are mandatory, how fresh KYC data must be. The attestor set is committed as a Merkle root (compact approved list). Once stored, policy terms are tamper-evident.

Run
make demo-setup
Grants ISSUER and EMPLOYEE roles, creates policy in PolicyRegistry, seeds Redis with compliance records and policy definition.
Watch for
Policy ID and hash committed on-chain
Attestor Merkle root — this binds the authorised set
Threshold = 1 (k-of-n quorum)
KYC_PASS, AML_PASS seeded in Redis for the employee
Step 2 of 5
make demo-request

Step 2: the issuer submits Alice's payment request.

Amount, beneficiary, privacy mode, and chain ID are hashed together and stored in the IssuanceRegistry. This commitment is immutable. Any change to any field — even one digit of the amount — produces a different hash, and the proof fails.

Run
make demo-request
Issuer calls createIssuanceRequest. Parameters hash is stored on-chain. Request ID is assigned.
Watch for
Request ID — note this for the next steps
Parameters hash — a binding commitment to amount, beneficiary, privacy mode
Status: REQUESTED — nothing executes until a proof arrives
Step 3 of 5
make demo-attest DEMO_REQUEST_ID=N

Step 3: the compliance officer signs the request off-chain.

The attestor signs the parameters hash with their ECDSA key and stores the signature in Redis. Nothing is published on-chain at this point. The circuit will later recover the signer's address from the signature and verify they are in the approved attestor list.

Run
make demo-attest DEMO_REQUEST_ID=N
Attestor signs the parameters hash. Signature and Merkle proof stored in Redis. No on-chain transaction.
Watch for
Attestor address and ECDSA signature (truncated)
Merkle leaf and proof — proves membership in the registered set
Redis key — compliance data never touches the chain
Step 4 of 5
make demo-status DEMO_REQUEST_ID=N

Step 4: the worker proves compliance, then settlement executes.

The worker assembles the witness — parameters, policy, signatures, KYC records — and passes it to the SP1 guest. If checks pass, a proof is produced and submitted to TokenisationEngine. The contract verifies the proof, reads the committed hashes, marks the request as consumed, and mints Alice's token.

Run
make demo-status DEMO_REQUEST_ID=N
Polls the worker until the proof job completes. Shows status transitions in real time.
Watch for
Status: RUNNING → COMPLETED (watch the worker pane)
Execution transaction hash — settlement on-chain
Token balance updated for Alice's beneficiary address
Step 5 of 5
make demo-audit DEMO_REQUEST_ID=N

Step 5: auditors can verify what happened without raw personal data.

The audit command surfaces the complete verifiable trail: request parameters, policy configuration, attestation records, proof job state, compliance check results. The ZK proof is on-chain and publicly verifiable. The compliance source records stay in Redis, off-chain, institution-controlled.

Summary

Institutions adopt when controls are enforceable, privacy is configurable, and audits are easy.

  • 1
    Configurable privacy — policy constrains disclosure; execution preserves confidentiality where required.
  • 2
    Off-chain evidence, on-chain verification — the chain learns only commitments and proof outputs. Compliance data never touches the chain.
  • 3
    Independent auditability — the proof and the identifiers are permanent; the underlying data remains controlled by the institution.

Eudemonia is compliance infrastructure: existing institutions keep their systems, and add verifiable policy enforcement.
